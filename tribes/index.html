The fourteen tribes

Merchant families who held Galway’s offices, traded wine, salt and hides with France and Spain, and built the town’s stone houses.

  • AthyAmong the earliest of the merchant families named in the town records.2 sites
  • BlakeTheir tower house still stands where Quay Street meets the river.4 sites
  • BodkinWardens of Saint Nicholas’s, with a lane named for them.3 sites
  • BrowneTheir carved doorway of 1627 now stands in Eyre Square.2 sites
  • D'ArcyA Gaelic family whose doorway still stands on Abbeygate Street.3 sites
  • DeaneMerchants and mayors through the 1500s and 1600s.2 sites
  • FontMerchants and mayors of Abbeygate Street, tied by marriage to the Lynches.1 site
  • FrenchSir Peter French built his mansion on Market Street.1 site
  • JoyceGave their name to Joyce Country; a Joyce vicar drew the town’s Pictorial Map.2 sites
  • KirwanRemembered in Kirwan’s Lane, one of the oldest streets in the city.3 sites
  • LynchBuilt Lynch’s Castle and gave Galway its first mayor in 1485.10 sites
  • MartinMillers on the river, with a tower on the town wall.5 sites
  • MorrisIn the town by the 1470s, buried at the Franciscan Abbey.1 site
  • SkerrettBrewers of Church Lane and householders of Bowling Green.2 sites
