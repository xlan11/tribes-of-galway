The fourteen tribes

Twelve Anglo-Norman families and two Gaelic Irish ones, who traded wine, salt and hides and governed Galway for two centuries.

  • AthyAmong the earliest of the merchant families named in the town records.
  • BlakeTheir tower house still stands where Quay Street meets the river.
  • BodkinA branch of the Fitzgeralds who took a new surname in Galway.
  • BrowneTheir carved doorway of 1627 now stands in Eyre Square.
  • D'ArcyOne of two Gaelic families among the fourteen.
  • DeaneMerchants and mayors through the 1500s and 1600s.
  • FontThe smallest of the tribes, gone from the city by the 1700s.
  • FrenchWine traders whose name came from their Norman roots.
  • JoyceGave their name to Joyce Country, and a goldsmith to the Claddagh ring.
  • KirwanRemembered in Kirwan’s Lane, one of the oldest streets in the city.
  • LynchHeld the mayoralty 84 times between 1485 and 1654.
  • MartinLater lords of Ballynahinch, and of “Humanity Dick” Martin.
  • MorrisMerchants who later held lands across County Galway.
  • SkerrettTraders on the quays from the 1300s.
