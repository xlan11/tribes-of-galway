Bodkin · Prospect Hill

Prospect Hill and Eyre Square

By the late 1800sthere were few Bodkins in Galway town. In Nun’s Island was labourer John Bodkin and his wives May Fallon and Louisa Burke while out in Oranmore was butcher Andrew Bodkin and his wife Mary Freney. Living in house 1 Prospect Hill on the night of 31 March 1901 was fiftyseven-year-old provision dealer Patrick Louis Bodkin and his forty-six-year-old wife, Winifred. Both were born in Galway town, spoke Irish and English, and were Catholic. Living with them was their eighteen-year-old son Leo Bodkin, a scholar, plus domestic servant Katie Joyce. On the 1911 census form Winifred stated she and Patrick were married thirty-five years and had four children, only one alive. This was twenty-nine-year-old Leo who was a law student living at 35 Saint Peter’s Road in Phibsborough, Dublin.

They were married in Dublin’s Marlboro Street cathedral on 29 March 1876. Patrick gave his address as Quay Street in Galway and his father as corn dealer Patrick Bodkin. Winifred was recorded as Minnie Pattie, daughter of mason Michael Pattie of Eyre Street in Galway. Father Bernard Farrell married them, their witnesses Timothy Dempsey and Helena Colclough, an in-law or cousin of Winifred. Why they were married in Dublin rather than Galway is uncertain.

Her father, Michael, died 18 April 1879 aged sixty-three; her likely grandfather, James, was ninety when he died in Galway during 1869. Later generations of the Paths are buried in Bohermore Cemetery.

Civil registers show that at Prospect Hill on 19 April 1882 Patrick Leo Bodkin was born to Winefred Path and shopkeeper Patrick Bodkin. On 9 April 1884

was born Mary Ellen Bodkin. Another was Michael (nicknamed Sonny), who died at their home aged nineteen on 7 February 1900. An unmarried clerk working for the local gas company, he died from some form of heart disease and would be unremarked save for a relationship he had in the final months of his life with another local, Nora Barnacle.

Nora’s mother was one of the Healy’s of 24 Upper Abbeygate Street West. Above the doorway is a cross date 1816 with the name John Healy. What little can now be said of Michael and Nora is due to the efforts of an tAthair Pádraig Ó Laoi, who investigated the matter when some of their contemporaries were still alive. Nora was fifteen and Michael was eighteen when they met, so met about 1898-99. Ó Laoí stated Nora was impressed by Michael’s “gentility and manliness” and visited the family sweetshop to “throw eyes” at Michael, chatting and flirting. According to Nora’s friend Mary O’Holleran Morris, Nora fell deeply in love with Michael. He was black-haired. Nora was auburn-haired. He gave her a present of a bracelet. She gave him her love. But whatever might have come to pass ended when Michael took ill and was confined to bed in the months before his death. Nora had already lost one boyfriend to premature death, Michael Feeney, and while tragic, it was a series of events both unfortunate and common.

None of this would matter any more than most teenage romances but for Nora meeting and being attracted to a man who resembled Michael Bodkin. On Dublin’s Nassau Street on 10 June 1904 she met James Joyce, and for the rest of her life and afterlife was defined by him.

Joyce fused Michael Feeney and Michael Bodkin into the lost love of the protagonist of his finest short story, The Dead. This fiction overpowered the boys on whom it is based, long since less than ghosts.

Winifred Path died 18 February 1915 aged sixty-five. Her husband Patrick Bodkin died 11 June 1928 aged eighty-five. Both were buried in Rahoon New Cemetery in an impressive above-ground vault. Its initial inscription reads: “Erected by Patrick L. and Winifred Bodkin in loving memory of their dearly beloved son Michael Maria who died on the 11th February 1900 aged 20 years. Lord Jesus deliver him. Comfortess of the afflicted intercede for him.” The Bodkin home and shop was sold and became John O’Loughlin’s drapery business, which he closed in July 1994 and is now part of Richardson’s Pub on the corner of Prospect Hill and Eyre Square. It is just yards from the Liam Mellows statue which stands on the site of the town gallows and where John fitz John Bodkin was executed on 20 March 1742. In January 2024 a plaque concerning Bodkin and The Dead was unveiled by President Higgins beside the family vault in Rahoon. Present was Michael Bodkin’s grandniece, Mrs. Mary O’Connor and her family.

From “The Tribes Experience of Galway & other notable families” by Adrian Martyn, edited by Pete Kelly.
