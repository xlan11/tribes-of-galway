Walk the old town

Four parts of the city where the tribes lived, traded and prayed, and what to find in each.

The Latin Quarter

Shop Street, Quay Street and the lanes between: the heart of the walled town.

  • Lynch’s CastleThe Lynch town house at Shop Street and Abbeygate Street, carved with the family arms.
  • Lynch Memorial WindowThe window behind St Nicholas’ where the Lynch hanging is said to have happened.
  • St Nicholas’ Collegiate ChurchThe parish church of the tribes, founded in 1320, with their tombs and arms inside.
  • Kirwan’s LaneA medieval lane named for the Kirwan family, between Quay Street and Cross Street.

Eyre Square

The green outside the old walls, with the Browne Doorway and the tribal flags.

  • Browne DoorwayThe doorway of the Browne house of 1627, rebuilt here in 1905.
  • Eyre SquareThe old green outside the walls, where the fourteen tribal flags fly today.

Spanish Arch and the quays

Where the wine ships came in, and the town wall met the river.

  • Blake’s CastleA tower house of the Blake family at the foot of Quay Street.
  • Hall of the Red EarlThe de Burgo hall where the town paid its taxes, before the tribes held power.
  • Spanish ArchPart of the city wall of 1584, where ships from France and Spain unloaded wine.

The Claddagh

The fishing village across the Corrib, with its own king and its own ring.

  • The CladdaghThe fishing village across the river, home of the ring Richard Joyce is said to have made.
